At a glance

The quarter in six points

  1. Staking grew. The two Reddex pools held 754.8 million RBNT at the end of the quarter, up from 639.1 million, a rise of 18.1%. Most of the rise was in the no lock pool, and most of that came in the week to 1 September 2026.
  2. Vesting contracts paid out. Contracts whose verified code is a vesting manager held 1.68 billion RBNT at the start and 1.58 billion at the end. They sent 127.8 million to wallets during the quarter.
  3. The largest wallets added, on balance. The 21 wallets that started the quarter with 10 million RBNT or more held 1.06 billion then and 1.09 billion at the end. 9 of them added more than 1%, 7 stayed within 1% and 5 reduced by more than 1%.
  4. Holdings stayed concentrated. The largest 100 wallets held 76.7% of all RBNT held by wallets at the end, against 75.8% at the start. 12 wallets hold half of it.
  5. Reserved wallets mostly moved RBNT between themselves. Ecosystem (Locked) sent 500.0 million to Ecosystem 2 (Unlocked), which sent 253.1 million on to Investors. Governance (Locked) sent 33.3 million to Governance (Unlocked). Only Ecosystem (Unlocked) sent RBNT outside the reserved group.
  6. A little more of the supply is now held by wallets. Wallets held 1 RBNT or more at 53,574 addresses on 30 September, 157 more than on 30 June. Together with their wrapped and staked RBNT they held 1.96 billion RBNT, up from 1.82 billion.
Read this first

This report describes what the public Redbelly chain shows. It is about trends, not people. It names only addresses that are already public: Redbelly's nine reserved wallets from the DAO treasury page, the Wrapped RBNT contract and the two Reddex staking pools. Routescan labels no exchange wallet on Redbelly, so none is named. Every other address appears only inside a group of at least five, never on its own.

The chain records that coins moved. It does not record why, or who controls an address. When a group's holdings fall, the coins left those addresses; that may be a sale, a move to another address of the same owner, or a deposit into a contract. This report makes no price forecast and gives no advice.

How to read the numbers

Start means block 3,025,809, the last block before midnight UTC at the end of 30 June 2026. End means block 3,209,414, the last block before midnight UTC at the end of 30 September 2026. Month-end and weekly figures use the last block before midnight UTC on those days. Amounts are in RBNT and rounded to whole coins; the data files keep the exact values. "Million" and "billion" are rounded to one or two decimals.

For every address we add three things: the RBNT it holds, the Wrapped RBNT (WRBNT) it holds and the RBNT it has staked in either Reddex pool. That way a holder who wraps or stakes is not counted as having sold. The RBNT sitting inside the WRBNT contract and the staked RBNT inside the pools is therefore counted once, with its owner.

Section 1

Every RBNT accounted for

All 10 billion RBNT were created in one allocation when the chain started. On 30 September we found 9,999,963,515 of them, 99.9996%, in the balances we read. Only 36,485 RBNT were not in any address we found.

A report about holders is only as good as its list of holders. Instead of trusting an explorer's list, we built our own: every address that sent or received a transaction or an internal transfer since the chain began, every address named in a block header, every address that ever touched WRBNT or a staking pool, and Routescan's own list of the largest addresses. That is 761,587 addresses. We then asked the Redbelly node for the balance of every one of them at each snapshot block.

Where network fees go. Senders paid 2.0 million RBNT in network fees during the quarter, yet the total in all balances changed by only 11 RBNT. The fees were not destroyed: the network credited 2.0 million RBNT of them to 219 addresses, without a transaction, as a change in their balance. Section 2

Who holds the RBNT

At the end of the quarter, 59.3% of all RBNT sat in Redbelly's reserved wallets, 19.6% with other wallets and 21.1% in other contracts. Most of the RBNT in contracts is in vesting contracts.

RBNT is the coin of the Redbelly network. Every coin sits at an address, and anyone can read the balance of any address. An address is either a wallet, controlled by a private key, or a contract, a program that holds coins under its own rules. Wrapped RBNT

The WRBNT supply fell from 1.82 billion to 1.74 billion: 38.7 million RBNT was wrapped and 120.8 million unwrapped. On both dates the supply equalled the RBNT held by the WRBNT contract and the sum of all WRBNT balances, to the last unit, so each WRBNT was backed by one RBNT. The contract's RBNT is counted once in this report, with whoever holds the WRBNT.

Exchanges

The brief asks for flows in and out of labelled exchange wallets. On 23 October 2026, Routescan's public address list for Redbelly mainnet gave a name to only one address, the Wrapped RBNT contract, among the 10,042 largest addresses it lists (down to a balance of 125 RBNT). It labelled no exchange wallet. With no public label to go by, this edition reports no exchange flows. Treating an unlabelled address as an exchange would be a guess about who controls it, which this report does not make. The method explains how the next edition picks up labels once Routescan publishes them.

Section 8

Method

Everything in this report comes from public chain data, read at fixed blocks, with scripts anyone can run. METHOD.md in the data package gives every call in detail; this is the short version.

  1. Fix the moments. For each date, find the last block before midnight UTC by binary search on block times from the Redbelly node, and check it against Routescan's getblocknobytime.
  2. Find every address. Read every transaction receipt from block 0 to block 3,209,414 (3,209,415 blocks), every block header, all 1,753,030 internal transfers from Routescan, every WRBNT and staking event, and Routescan's address list. This gives 761,587 addresses.
  3. Read balances. Ask the node for the RBNT balance of every address at each month-end block, the WRBNT balance of every address that ever held WRBNT, and the stake of every address that ever staked.
  4. Count each coin once. Add each address's RBNT, WRBNT and staked RBNT. Leave out the WRBNT contract's RBNT (it backs the WRBNT) and the staked part of each pool (it belongs to the stakers). The rest of each pool's balance is its reward funds.
  5. Sort addresses. Named addresses are reported by name. Every other address is a wallet (no code) or a contract (has code at the end block). Contracts are grouped by the name in their verified code, only where five or more share a kind.
  6. Follow movements. Take every transaction and internal transfer between the two blocks, and every WRBNT transfer, and add them up by group. Check that every named address reconciles to the last unit.
  7. Check. The scripts stop if any of the checks below fails. A separate script then re-reads the headline figures from the node and scans every file for anything the brief forbids.
Pool balances include reward funds

Rewards in both Reddex pools are paid in RBNT from the pool's own balance, so a pool balance may include reward funds as well as stakes. The headline staking figures use the pools' RBNT balances, as the brief asks. The split into staked RBNT and reward funds uses the pools' own totalStake.

Checks that passed on this data

57 checks in all, listed one by one in figures.json. Each is an exact equality in wei.

  • ✓ WRBNT totalSupply equals RBNT held by the WRBNT contract (4 times)
  • ✓ Sum of WRBNT balances equals WRBNT totalSupply (4 times)
  • ✓ Stakers' amounts add up to totalStake (8 times)
  • ✓ Where-RBNT-sits groups add up to 10 billion (4 times)
  • ✓ Ownership groups add up to 10 billion (4 times)
  • ✓ Cohort start total matches (5 times)
  • ✓ Cohort end total matches (5 times)
  • ✓ Contract groups plus contracts under 1 RBNT add up to all contracts at the end
  • ✓ No followed wallet became a contract during the quarter
  • ✓ Net changes of all groups add up to zero
  • ✓ Named address reconciles to the wei (12 times)
  • ✓ Plain transfers into the pool equal money in minus stakes (2 times)
  • ✓ Stakes minus unstakes equal the change in totalStake (2 times)
  • ✓ Lock calendar adds up to totalStake at the end (2 times)
  • ✓ No address lost RBNT that no transfer or fee explains
  • ✓ Fee credits are no larger than the fees paid

What the chain does not show

Who controls an address, why coins moved, and whether a transfer was a sale. This report describes movements only.

Words used

Address
A public account on the chain, written as 0x followed by 40 characters.
Wallet
An address controlled by a private key. It holds no code.
Contract
An address that runs code, such as a staking pool, a vesting contract or a multi-signature wallet.
Block
A batch of transactions. Each block has a number and a time, so a block number fixes a moment.
Staking
Putting RBNT into a contract to earn rewards.
WRBNT
Wrapped RBNT, a token backed one to one by RBNT held in the WRBNT contract, used by apps.
Vesting contract
